Brazil SPA opens consultation on betting authorisation rules

Brazil's SPA has opened a 45-day consultation to define commercial authorisation rules for fixed-odds betting, shaping the market's next licensing phase.

By GamblingLawyers.com Editorial Team
24 July 2026

Brazil's Secretariat of Prizes and Betting (SPA), the wagering unit within the Ministry of Finance, has published a public-consultation notice in the Federal Official Gazette seeking input on the ordinance that will govern commercial authorisation for the fixed-odds betting lottery modality. Running for 45 days from 27 July 2026 until 9 September 2026, the consultation invites contributions from any individual or legal entity through the Brasil Participativo portal, and it marks the next substantive step in the maturation of a market that only formally opened to licensed operators at the start of 2025.

The legal significance of the consultation lies in its subject matter. Brazil's initial licensing round under Law 14.790/2023 established the gateway conditions for market entry, but the day-to-day contours of ongoing commercial authorisation have remained partially unsettled. By consulting specifically on the authorisation ordinance, the SPA is signalling that it intends to codify the operational rules governing how authorisations are granted, maintained, and potentially varied, rather than leaving those questions to be resolved case by case through administrative practice. For operators, that promises greater predictability, but only once the final text emerges.

The timing is telling. As of the end of June 2026, 87 operators held active federal licences with a further 14 applications pending, a level of market participation that gives the SPA a substantial regulated base whose experience can inform the rulemaking. A consultation launched at this juncture allows the regulator to calibrate authorisation requirements against real operational data rather than pre-launch speculation, and it gives incumbents a direct channel to shape rules that will bind their competitors as much as themselves.

Operators and their advisers should approach the consultation as a genuine opportunity rather than a formality. Brazilian administrative law affords consultation responses real weight where they are substantiated, and the SPA has committed to analysing every submission after the window closes. Well-evidenced contributions on questions such as corporate-structure disclosure, the treatment of controlling shareholders, and the mechanics of authorisation renewal are far more likely to influence the final ordinance than generalised expressions of preference. Firms with established Brazilian gaming practices are already coordinating client submissions.

The consultation must also be read against the broader tightening of the Brazilian regime. Mandatory advertising warnings and stricter promotional rules took effect earlier in July 2026, and the SPA has maintained its firm prohibition on cryptocurrency deposits and withdrawals from licensed platforms. The authorisation ordinance will not exist in isolation; it will interlock with these advertising and payment rules to form an increasingly comprehensive compliance perimeter that operators must navigate as a whole rather than as discrete silos.

For prospective entrants still weighing the Brazilian opportunity, the consultation is a valuable early-warning system. The draft parameters the SPA puts forward will reveal how demanding the ongoing authorisation burden is likely to be, and that in turn bears directly on the cost of doing business and the viability of thinner-margin propositions. Entities that monitor the consultation closely will be better positioned to model their compliance overhead before committing capital to a licence application.

The overarching message is that Brazil is moving from the improvisation of a newly opened market toward the settled predictability of a mature regulatory regime. The authorisation ordinance, once finalised, will be a cornerstone of that transition. Operators that treat the consultation as an inflection point, and that engage substantively while the rules remain fluid, will be best placed to thrive under the framework that emerges on the other side of the 9 September deadline.
