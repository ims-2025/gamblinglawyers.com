Dutch licence renewals loom as KSA raises fees

The first Dutch remote licences expire on 30 September 2026 just as fees jump — turning renewal into a full compliance re-examination.

By GamblingLawyers.com Editorial Team
1 August 2026

The Netherlands is approaching the first true test of its regulated online market since it opened in October 2021. The earliest remote gambling licences, granted for a five-year term, expire on 30 September 2026, obliging the first cohort of operators to seek renewal from the Kansspelautoriteit (KSA) against a regulatory backdrop that has changed markedly since they were first admitted. Renewal is not a formality: it is an opportunity for the regulator to reassess whether operators that were licensed on the basis of promises have delivered on them in practice.

The commercial terms of participation have moved as well. From April 2026 the fee for a new remote gambling licence rose from €48,000 to €61,300, and the fee for modifying an existing licence increased from €8,000 to €10,200. Those figures are modest relative to the cost of operating in the market, but they signal a regulator that expects to be resourced for more intensive supervision, and they raise the administrative cost of the corporate and product changes that operators routinely make between licence terms.

What makes the renewal wave significant is the distance travelled on player protection since 2021. The KSA has tightened its expectations on the duty of care, introduced deposit-limit and affordability considerations, and made clear that it regards the monitoring of excessive play as a continuing obligation rather than a box-ticking exercise at onboarding. An operator seeking renewal will be judged not against the standards that applied when it entered, but against the standards that apply now, and against its own record of intervention over the intervening years.

That retrospective quality is the heart of the legal challenge. Renewal effectively invites the KSA to examine the operator's compliance history, including any interventions, warnings or informal criticisms accumulated during the licence term. Operators with a clean record can approach the process with confidence; those that have attracted regulatory attention on advertising, duty of care or anti-money-laundering matters face the prospect of that history being weighed in the balance. Preparing a renewal file is, in practice, preparing a defence of five years of conduct.

The KSA has also signalled a broader enforcement posture that colours the renewal environment. It has committed to intensify action against illegal gambling, expanded anti-money-laundering supervision to reach payment providers and business-to-business suppliers, and set out an agenda focused on vulnerable players, advertising and Wwft compliance. An operator that is comfortably compliant on its own licence may still find its commercial partners drawn into scrutiny, and renewal is a natural moment for the regulator to probe the integrity of the operator's wider ecosystem.

For operators, the practical response is to treat renewal as a full compliance audit conducted twelve months ahead of expiry. That means reconstructing the evidence trail for duty-of-care interventions, ensuring that affordability and anti-money-laundering controls reflect current expectations, and resolving any open regulatory matters before they harden into obstacles. It also means budgeting for the higher fees and for the legal cost of a substantive submission rather than a renewal form. Leaving the exercise to the final quarter is the principal avoidable risk.

The wider significance is what the renewal cohort will reveal about the maturity of the Dutch model. A market that admitted operators on generous terms is now testing whether continued participation must be earned through demonstrated compliance. How the KSA handles this first wave, whether it renews smoothly, imposes conditions, or declines in hard cases, will set the template for every cohort that follows, and will tell international operators a great deal about the true cost of holding a Dutch licence over the long term.
