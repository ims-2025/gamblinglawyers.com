Brazil consults on permanent betting licences

Brazil's SPA has opened a consultation to set permanent commercial-authorisation rules for fixed-odds operators as it shifts to enforcement.

By GamblingLawyers.com Editorial Team
28 July 2026

Brazil's Secretariat of Prizes and Betting has opened a 45-day public consultation, running from 27 July to 9 September 2026, to fix the permanent rules for commercial authorisation of fixed-odds betting operators. The consultation marks the transition from the transitional licensing that opened the regulated market at the start of 2025 toward a durable framework, and it arrives as the SPA pivots decisively from market-building to supervision and enforcement.

The timing reflects a market that has matured faster than many observers expected. As of 30 June 2026, 87 operators held active federal licences with a further 14 applications pending, a level of participation that gives the regulator both the data and the confidence to codify permanent terms. For operators that entered during the transitional phase, the consultation is the moment at which the provisional becomes settled, and the assumptions on which early market-entry decisions were made will be tested against rules designed for the long term.

Substantively, the reform agenda has moved toward integrity and reporting. The SPA has centred its 2026 work on market integrity and full integration with the SIGAP portal for real-time reporting, and it initiated 66 supervisory proceedings during early oversight. Operators must consult SIGAP when a user first registers or logs in, using the individual's CPF number to check whether that person is flagged under Brazil's centralised self-exclusion system. Permanent authorisation is likely to harden these obligations from operational practice into licence conditions.

The fiscal backdrop sharpens the calculus. The gross-gaming-revenue tax, set at 12 percent at launch, rose to 13 percent in July 2026, with further increases possible as the market develops. For operators weighing whether to convert transitional participation into a permanent commitment, the interaction between a rising tax base and the fixed costs of compliance infrastructure will determine which business models remain viable. A market that looked attractive at 12 percent may look different once permanent obligations and a climbing rate are priced in together.

Advertising has become a parallel front. SPA Ordinance No. 1,964/2026, in force from 3 July 2026, requires betting advertisements to carry standardised warning messages, extending consumer-protection logic into the marketing layer. For a market that grew on aggressive customer acquisition, the codification of mandatory warnings signals that the regulator intends to police not only who may operate but how they may communicate, and permanent-authorisation applicants will need advertising-compliance frameworks that match.

The consultation is also an exercise in regulatory competition. Brazil is one of the largest newly regulated markets in the world, and the terms it settles now will influence whether international operators treat it as a core market or a peripheral one. A framework that couples heavy reporting and rising duty with legal certainty may still prove attractive, because certainty itself has value; a framework that adds obligation without clarity risks pushing volume back toward the unlicensed offshore operators the regime was designed to displace.

For legal advisers, including leading Brazilian firms such as Pinheiro Neto Advogados and Mattos Filho, the consultation is a rare opportunity to shape binding rules rather than merely comply with them. Operators would be well advised to treat their submissions as substantive advocacy, addressing the practical workability of SIGAP integration, the proportionality of self-exclusion checks, and the cumulative weight of tax and compliance costs. The permanent regime that emerges will define market entry in Brazil for years, and the window to influence it is measured in weeks.
