Missouri sports betting settles into its licence regime

Missouri's sportsbooks are bedding in under a 14-licence online cap and 10% revenue tax overseen by the Missouri Gaming Commission.

By GamblingLawyers.com Editorial Team
29 July 2026

Missouri's sports-betting market has moved past the drama of legalisation into the quieter and more consequential phase of licensed operation. Betting launched on 1 December 2025, roughly a year after Amendment 2 passed at the November 2024 ballot with a margin of just 50.1 percent. The narrowness of that vote is worth remembering, because a market created by the thinnest of majorities carries an implicit political fragility: the regulatory choices made in the early months will shape whether the regime is seen as a success or as a mistake that opponents can reopen.

The licensing structure codified in the enabling law is deliberately bounded. Missouri authorised up to 14 online sportsbook licences and 19 retail licences, distributed among the state's 13 casinos and six professional sports teams, with two additional online-only licences reserved for operators lacking a casino or team affiliation. Those two untethered licences were awarded to DraftKings and Circa in the run-up to launch, a detail that matters because it determined which national operators could enter without a local partner. The cap on online licences means Missouri is a market of managed scarcity rather than open access.

That scarcity has legal and commercial consequences. In a capped market, the licence itself is a valuable and contested asset, and the tie between casinos, teams and mobile skins channels access through incumbent relationships. Operators without a casino or team partner competed for the two direct mobile licences precisely because those were the only routes into the market that did not require a local counterparty. The structure rewards early positioning and existing Missouri relationships, and it will frame any future dispute over how additional access, if any, is allocated.

Oversight rests with the Missouri Gaming Commission, the established regulator for the state's riverboat casino industry, now extended into online and retail sports wagering. Housing sports betting within an existing gaming regulator rather than a purpose-built body brings the advantage of institutional experience but also imports the Commission's existing supervisory culture and enforcement posture. Operators familiar with the Commission's approach to casino compliance can expect that same rigour applied to sportsbook conduct, from responsible-gambling controls to advertising and integrity monitoring.

The fiscal design is modest by national standards and politically pointed. Missouri levies a 10 percent tax on sportsbook gross revenue, with proceeds directed to K-12 public education and to problem-gambling programs. A 10 percent rate sits well below the levels seen in some other states, a choice that keeps the licensed product competitive but limits the revenue the state can claim from it. The earmarking to education and treatment is characteristic of ballot-initiative gambling regimes, where the promise of a public benefit is often what secured the votes needed to pass.

The early operating period is where the durable questions surface. Eligible users aged 21 and over may bet online within state lines and at approved retail locations, and the interaction between geolocation, age verification and the capped licence structure will test the Commission's supervisory reach in practice. As the market settles, attention will turn to whether the 14-licence online ceiling proves adequate to demand, how retail and mobile access interact for casino licensees, and whether the modest tax rate holds or comes under pressure as other budget needs compete.

For legal advisers, including US gaming practices such as Ifrah Law, Jones Walker and Greenberg Traurig, Missouri's transition from launch to steady-state operation generates licensing, compliance and disputes work in equal measure. Clients will need guidance on the mechanics of the capped licence regime, on satisfying the Missouri Gaming Commission's compliance expectations, and on positioning for any future expansion of access. In a market born of a razor-thin vote and built on managed scarcity, the operators that treat regulatory relationships as a core asset will be the ones that endure.
