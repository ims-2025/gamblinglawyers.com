Brazil consults on fixed-odds betting authorisation

The SPA's Public Consultation No. 3/2026 reopens the authorisation rules for fixed-odds betting, giving new entrants a rare chance to shape the regime.

By GamblingLawyers.com Editorial Team
31 July 2026

Brazil's Secretaria de Premios e Apostas has published Public Consultation Notice SPA/MF No. 3/2026 in the Federal Official Gazette, opening a 45-day window, running from late July to early September, for submissions on the rules and conditions governing authorisation to operate the fixed-odds betting lottery modality. Coming after the frantic authorisation grant that established the licensed market, the consultation signals that Brazil's regulator is now willing to refine the entry framework rather than treat the initial rules as settled.

The timing matters because the first cohort of authorisations was granted under considerable time pressure. The federal regime created by Law 14,790/2023 required the SPA to stand up an entire licensing apparatus, define technical standards, and process a large volume of applications within a compressed statutory timetable. The result was a workable framework built at speed. A consultation on the authorisation rules is the regulator's opportunity to address the practical problems that surfaced during that first round, and prospective entrants that engage now can influence conditions they will later have to live under.

For operators that missed the initial authorisation window, the consultation is significant. As of the end of June, the licensed market comprised dozens of authorised operators with further applications pending, and the market has continued to attract international brands drawn by Brazil's scale. The rules that emerge from this consultation will determine how straightforward it is for later entrants to obtain a federal authorisation, including the corporate, financial and technical requirements they must satisfy and the fees they must pay. Latecomers have a direct interest in ensuring the framework does not entrench the advantages of first movers.

The substantive questions the consultation is likely to engage include the treatment of the substantial authorisation fee, the requirements around Brazilian corporate presence and local shareholding, the financial guarantees operators must post, and the technical certification standards for betting systems. Each of these has been a point of friction in the first phase. Counsel advising prospective entrants should treat the consultation as a live opportunity to make reasoned submissions on the practical operation of these requirements, supported by comparative regulatory experience from other jurisdictions that have licensed fixed-odds betting at scale.

The consultation also has implications for the operators already holding authorisations. Changes to the entry rules can alter the competitive dynamics of the market by making it easier or harder for new competitors to arrive. Incumbents that invested heavily to meet the first-round requirements have a legitimate interest in ensuring that later entrants face comparable obligations, and they too should participate in the consultation. The SPA will be weighing the competing interests of market development, consumer protection and revenue generation, and the operators best served will be those whose submissions engage with that balance rather than simply advocating their own commercial position.

The broader context is that Brazil's regime is transitioning from establishment to maturity. The authorisation phase is giving way to a period of active supervision and enforcement, including the blocking of unlicensed domains and the tightening of advertising rules through instruments such as the ordinance restricting urgency-based marketing language. A consultation on the authorisation framework fits this arc: the regulator is stabilising the rules of entry at the same time as it hardens the boundary between the licensed and unlicensed markets. The two efforts are complementary, and both point toward a more predictable regulatory environment.

For counsel, the practical steps are straightforward. Prospective entrants should prepare substantive submissions before the September deadline, addressing the specific authorisation requirements that bear on their entry strategy. Existing authorisation holders should review the consultation for any proposed changes that would affect their competitive position or their compliance obligations. And all participants should recognise that the Brazilian framework remains in flux; the operators that engage constructively with the SPA during this formative period will be better placed than those that wait for the rules to be imposed. A rare open door to shape a major market does not stay open for long.
