Finland opens its gambling market to competition

Finland's licensing reform ends Veikkaus's digital monopoly, opening betting and online casino to licensed competition from July 2027.

By GamblingLawyers.com Editorial Team
29 July 2026

Finland is dismantling one of the last state gambling monopolies in Western Europe, and the process is now irreversible. Parliament approved a new Gambling Act on 16 December 2025, replacing the framework that had reserved digital betting and online casino play for the state-owned operator Veikkaus. The B2C application window opened on 1 March 2026, the first licences are expected to be granted by the end of 2026, and the regulated market will formally open to competition on 1 July 2027. For operators that have spent years channelling Finnish demand through offshore sites, the reform converts a grey market into a licensed one on a fixed timetable.

The break with the past is genuine but deliberately partial. The reform ends Veikkaus's exclusive hold on online betting and online casino games, yet the operator retains monopoly rights over lotteries, scratch cards and certain land-based products. The result is a hybrid system rather than a clean liberalisation, and that structure will shape competitive strategy: private licensees will compete in the newly opened verticals while the incumbent keeps a protected core. Advisers will need to map precisely where the monopoly ends and the licensed market begins, because the boundary defines what a new entrant may lawfully offer.

The licensing architecture is built around three categories that separate the layers of the industry. B2C operators, B2B platform providers and game developers each require their own authorisation, a design that pushes accountability up the supply chain rather than concentrating it solely on the consumer-facing brand. Applicants must demonstrate financial stability, responsible-gambling frameworks, anti-money-laundering controls and technical testing of their platforms, and the process is staged so that a general application and processing fee precede a deeper assessment of the technical and responsible-gambling systems.

Institutional responsibility is also in transition, and the handover matters for anyone timing an entry. The National Police Board remains the responsible authority until 30 June 2027, after which a dedicated supervisory agency assumes licensing and enforcement duties as the market opens. From 1 July 2028 a further condition bites: licensed operators must use software supplied by licensed providers, closing the loop between the B2C and B2B licence categories. Operators must therefore plan not just for the 2027 opening but for a 2028 supply-chain requirement that will constrain their choice of technical partners.

The policy logic is channelisation, the same anxiety driving reform across the Nordic region. A monopoly that could not capture online demand left a large share of play with unlicensed operators beyond the reach of Finnish consumer protection and taxation. By licensing competition, the state aims to draw that activity back inside a supervised perimeter where advertising, affordability and self-exclusion rules apply. The reform also opens marketing and advertising opportunities that were previously closed, giving licensed operators a lawful route to acquire customers that the monopoly model denied them.

The commercial prize is significant, but so is the sequencing risk. First-mover advantage in a newly opened market is real, and operators that secure early licences will build brand recognition before the field fills. Yet the phased application process rewards preparation over speed: an operator that submits an incomplete file, or that cannot evidence its responsible-gambling and AML systems to the standard the authority expects, may find itself delayed past the July 2027 opening. The gap between application and market entry is where legal and compliance groundwork will decide winners and losers.

For legal advisers, including Nordic and international practices such as Mannheimer Swartling, Bird & Bird and CMS, the Finnish opening is a rare greenfield mandate in a mature European market. Clients will need help interpreting the boundary of Veikkaus's residual monopoly, structuring B2C and B2B applications, and preparing for the 2028 licensed-software requirement. The firms that master the sequencing of Finland's phased reform will be positioned to guide operators into one of the few genuinely new regulated markets Western Europe has produced this decade.
