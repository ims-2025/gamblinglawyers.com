Germany reviews gambling treaty as stakes rise

Germany begins its first review of the 2021 gambling treaty as new tiered slot-stake limits take effect and the GGL changes leadership.

By GamblingLawyers.com Editorial Team
28 July 2026

Germany has entered a pivotal phase for its online gambling regime as the country prepares the first broad review of the Interstate Treaty on Gambling since it took effect on 1 July 2021. Five years on, the assessment will scrutinise advertising standards, deposit controls, consumer safeguards and the overall effectiveness of responsible-gambling measures, and its conclusions will shape whether the treaty's cautious, protection-first architecture survives intact or bends toward the commercial realities of channelisation.

The review coincides with a change at the top of the regulator. Christian Hochgrebe, Berlin's state secretary for the interior, took over as chairman of the administrative board of the Gemeinsame Gluecksspielbehoerde der Laender on 1 July 2026, succeeding Bavaria's Sandro Kirchner. Leadership transitions at the outset of a foundational review are rarely incidental; the incoming chair will help set the tone for how ambitiously the treaty is reopened, and stakeholders across the sixteen states will read early signals closely.

The most tangible change for operators is the recalibration of slot-stake limits. From 1 July 2026 the flat one-euro cap per spin gave way to a tiered system that varies with a player's age and gambling history, with a higher five-euro stake available in defined circumstances while players under 21 remain held to the one-euro maximum. The shift is a notable concession to the argument that overly rigid limits push players toward unlicensed sites, and it recasts the German model from uniform restriction toward risk-differentiated control.

That move responds to a persistent anxiety about channelisation. The regulated market generated 14.4 billion euros in gross gaming revenue in 2025, repeating the 2024 figure and suggesting the legal sector has plateaued at what may be its ceiling under the old constraints. A stable but stagnant licensed market, set against a resilient black market, is precisely the evidence that fuels arguments for loosening product restrictions, and the stake reform can be read as a first, measured test of whether liberalisation can grow the regulated share without eroding consumer protection.

Enforcement capability is being reinforced in step with the policy shift. The GGL has confirmed it will continue expanding LUGAS, the centralised cross-operator monitoring platform, in partnership with the public-sector technology provider Dataport. A more capable monitoring system matters most precisely when limits are being relaxed: differentiated stakes and history-based controls are only enforceable if the regulator can see and reconcile player activity across operators in something close to real time. The LUGAS expansion is the technical precondition for the policy loosening.

The interplay between the treaty review, the stake changes and the LUGAS build-out reveals a regulator trying to hold two goals in tension. It wants to make the licensed product attractive enough to draw players away from unlicensed sites, while retaining the data infrastructure and consumer safeguards that justify the regime's protective claims. Whether those goals are reconcilable is the central question the review must answer, and the answer will influence reform debates well beyond Germany's borders.

For legal advisers, including specialist gambling practices such as Hambach & Hambach, Redeker Sellner Dahs and CMS Germany, the review period is one of both uncertainty and opportunity. Operators will seek guidance on how to exploit the new stake tiers without falling foul of the age and history conditions, how to prepare for expanded LUGAS reporting, and how to engage with a treaty review whose outcome could redraw the market. In a federal system where sixteen states must align, the process will be slow, contested and consequential, and early engagement is likely to pay off.
