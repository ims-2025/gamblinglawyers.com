Australia moves to ban live-sport gambling ads

Australia's Gambling Reform Bill would ban online betting ads during live sport and force BetStop checks at login from January 2027.

By GamblingLawyers.com Editorial Team
29 July 2026

Australia has moved from consultation to legislation on gambling advertising, and the shift is consequential. The Interactive Gambling Amendment (Gambling Reform) Bill 2026, together with an associated National Self-exclusion Register cost-recovery measure, was introduced in Parliament on 2 July 2026, with the reforms scheduled to commence on 1 January 2027. After years of debate over the saturation of betting advertising in Australian sport, the government has committed to a statutory response rather than the industry self-regulation that has characterised the market to date.

The headline measure is a ban on all online gambling advertising during live sport broadcasts. The prohibition runs from one hour before play begins to one hour after it ends, and it reaches across television, radio, streaming platforms and in-stadium digital signage. Pre-match and post-match advertising windows will be restricted to after 10 p.m. local time, effectively removing gambling promotion from primetime sports coverage. The design targets the moments of highest exposure, and its breadth across broadcast and digital channels signals an intent to close the substitution routes that narrower bans have left open elsewhere.

Running alongside the advertising restrictions is a substantial strengthening of BetStop, the national self-exclusion register launched in August 2023. The reform package allocates an additional A$28 million to expand the register's capabilities and streamlines registration so that consumers can sign up through the myGov digital services portal, cutting the current processing period toward near-instant activation. For a self-exclusion tool to be effective, the friction of enrolment matters, and moving sign-up into a widely used government portal is a deliberate attempt to lower that barrier.

The most legally significant change may be the least visible to the public. Licensed operators will be required to conduct BetStop checks at login rather than only at account creation. That closes a long-criticised loophole under which a person who self-excluded after opening an account could continue to use it, because the operator never re-checked the register against existing customers. Shifting the obligation to the point of login converts self-exclusion from a one-time gate into a continuing duty, and it will require operators to re-engineer authentication flows to query the register on every session.

The government has framed the reforms around harm to young people. Communications Minister Michelle Rowland estimated that the advertising changes will cut gambling-ad exposure for Australians under 25 by roughly 80 percent. That framing is strategically important, because it anchors the measures in child and youth protection, a footing that is harder to contest than a general restriction on commercial speech. Operators and broadcasters that oppose the bill will have to argue against a benchmark expressed in terms of protecting minors, which narrows the space for a purely commercial objection.

For operators and the sports and media businesses that depend on gambling revenue, the reform reshapes the economics of Australian sport. Broadcast rights and sponsorship models built on betting advertising will need rebuilding before the January 2027 commencement, and the compressed timeline leaves limited room for transitional arrangements. The login-check requirement adds a technical compliance project on top of the commercial adjustment, and operators that treat the two as separate workstreams risk missing the deadline on one while managing the other.

For legal advisers, including firms with strong Australian and cross-border practices such as Herbert Smith Freehills Kramer, K&L Gates and Bird & Bird, the bill generates work across regulatory, media and technology disciplines at once. Clients will need advice on the reach of the advertising ban across streaming and digital signage, on renegotiating sponsorship and broadcast contracts, and on building login-stage BetStop integration that satisfies the new continuing duty. With commencement fixed for 1 January 2027, the window to prepare is measured in months, not years.
